Growth KPIs and Analytics: Measure What Actually Matters

Choose the right growth KPIs and set up analytics: a north-star metric, leading vs lagging indicators, events, conversions, UTMs and a weekly dashboard.

GGrow.Media Editorial Team·Updated October 30, 2026·8 min read
On this pageKey takeawaysStart with goals, not toolsChoosing your north-star metricLeading versus lagging indicatorsAnalytics basics: events, conversions, and UTMsBuilding your weekly dashboardTurning numbers into decisionsYour next step

Most small teams do not suffer from too little data; they suffer from too many numbers and no clear sense of which ones matter. Good measurement starts with a few deliberate choices about what success looks like, then builds just enough tracking to see it. This guide helps you pick a north-star metric, separate leading from lagging indicators, set up clean tracking, and run a weekly dashboard.

Key takeaways

  • Choose one north-star metric that captures the value customers get and that predicts revenue.
  • Pair lagging indicators (results) with leading indicators (activities and early signals) you can influence this week.
  • Define events and conversions in a written tracking plan before you configure any tool.
  • Tag every campaign link with consistent UTM parameters so you can trust channel reporting.
  • Review a short weekly dashboard and turn every notable change into a decision or experiment.

Start with goals, not tools

Before opening any analytics tool, write down what you are trying to achieve this quarter in one or two sentences. For example: "Grow paid course sales from our email list while keeping cost per subscriber stable." Every KPI should trace back to a goal like this. If a number cannot change a decision, it does not belong on your dashboard.

Then decide what kind of numbers you need:

Type What it tells you Examples
North-star metric Whether the business is delivering core value Weekly active customers, paid subscribers
Lagging indicators Results that have already happened Revenue, profit, churn rate
Leading indicators Early signals that predict results Trial signups, qualified leads, email replies
Health metrics Guardrails that should not get worse Refund rate, unsubscribe rate, page speed

Choosing your north-star metric

A north-star metric is the single number that best captures the value customers get from you. When it grows, the business generally grows. A good north star:

  1. Reflects customer value, not just your activity.
  2. Predicts revenue over time.
  3. Can be influenced by your team's work.
  4. Is easy to understand for anyone on the team.

Examples by business type

Business Possible north-star metric
YouTube creator Weekly watch time from returning viewers
Newsletter Engaged subscribers (opened at least one email in 30 days)
Local service business Booked jobs per week
Online store Repeat purchasers per month
Software tool Weekly active accounts completing a core action

Avoid north stars that can rise while the business gets worse. Total followers, for example, can grow while engagement and sales decline.

Leading versus lagging indicators

Lagging indicators such as revenue are the truth, but they arrive too late to steer by. Leading indicators let you act earlier.

To find your leading indicators, work backward from a lagging result:

  1. Write the lagging result, such as monthly revenue.
  2. Ask what has to happen before it: purchases.
  3. Ask what comes before purchases: checkout starts, sales calls, or trial signups.
  4. Keep going until you reach something you can influence this week, such as qualified leads or content published.

Map these stages with the AARRR growth funnel, then pick two or three leading indicators from the earlier stages.

Analytics basics: events, conversions, and UTMs

Events

An event is any tracked action: a page view, button click, form submission, video play, or purchase. Most modern analytics tools are event-based, which means you decide which actions matter and name them.

Conversions

A conversion is an event you have marked as valuable, such as a signup or purchase. Keep the list short. If everything is a conversion, nothing is.

Writing a tracking plan

Create a simple spreadsheet before you configure anything:

Event name Trigger Key details captured Conversion?
sign_up Email form submitted form location, lead magnet Yes
begin_checkout Checkout page loaded product, price No
purchase Order confirmation shown order value, product Yes
book_call Booking confirmed call type Yes
video_play Embedded video started video title No

Rules for a clean plan:

  1. Use one naming convention, such as lowercase with underscores, everywhere.
  2. Name events for what the user did, not where the button sits.
  3. Capture details as parameters rather than creating a new event for every variation.
  4. Test every event after setup by performing the action yourself and confirming it appears.

UTM parameters

UTM parameters are tags added to a link so your analytics can tell where a visit came from. The three you should always set are:

  • utm_source: the specific site or sender, such as newsletter or instagram.
  • utm_medium: the type of channel, such as email, social, or cpc.
  • utm_campaign: the specific campaign, such as spring_launch.

Two optional tags, utm_content and utm_term, help distinguish ad variations or keywords. Consistency is everything: "Email," "email," and "e-mail" show up as three different sources. Build every link with the UTM builder and keep a shared log of the values you use.

Never add UTM parameters to internal links on your own site, because doing so can overwrite the original source of the visit.

Building your weekly dashboard

Your weekly dashboard should fit on one screen. A practical structure:

Section Metric This week Last week 4-week trend
North star Engaged subscribers
Leading New signups
Leading Signup rate from top landing page
Leading Content pieces published
Lagging Revenue
Lagging New customers
Efficiency Cost per acquisition
Health Unsubscribe rate

A spreadsheet is enough to start. Update it at the same time each week, and include a short notes column explaining unusual movements, such as a holiday, a viral post, or a tracking issue.

To check whether channels are paying back their cost, run monthly totals through the marketing ROI calculator and add the result to a monthly view.

Turning numbers into decisions

A dashboard only matters if it changes what you do. In your weekly review:

  1. Look at the north star first. Is it moving in the right direction?
  2. Check leading indicators. Which one explains the change?
  3. Flag any health metric that worsened.
  4. Write one sentence for each notable change: what happened and why you think so.
  5. Assign one action or experiment for the coming week.

Watch for common traps. Small weekly numbers swing randomly, so compare four-week rolling totals before reacting. Correlation is not causation; two lines moving together may share a third cause. And be wary of vanity metrics that feel good but do not connect to your goal.

Your next step

Write your quarterly goal in one sentence and choose a north-star metric using the four criteria above. Draft a tracking plan with no more than five conversion events, set them up, and test each one yourself. Then build the weekly dashboard in a spreadsheet, fill in last week's numbers, and schedule a recurring 30-minute review at the same time every week.

Frequently asked questions

How many KPIs should a small business track?

Keep your weekly dashboard to roughly five to ten numbers: one north-star metric, a handful of leading indicators, and a few lagging results. More than that tends to scatter attention. You can still collect detailed data, but reserve the dashboard for numbers that drive decisions.

What is the difference between a metric and a KPI?

A metric is any number you can measure, such as page views or follower count. A KPI, or key performance indicator, is a metric you have chosen because it reflects progress toward a specific goal. Every KPI is a metric, but most metrics should not be KPIs.

Do I need expensive analytics software?

No. Free web analytics tools, your platform dashboards, and a spreadsheet cover most needs for creators and small businesses. Invest in paid tools only when a specific question cannot be answered with what you have. Clean tracking matters far more than the tool.

What if my numbers are too small to be meaningful?

With small numbers, look at longer time windows such as four-week rolling totals, and focus on direction rather than exact percentages. Pair the numbers with qualitative signals like customer conversations and replies. As volume grows, you can tighten the time frame.
