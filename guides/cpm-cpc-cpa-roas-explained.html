CPM, CPC, CPA and ROAS Explained With Worked Examples

CPM, CPC, CPA and ROAS explained with plain formulas and worked examples, plus break-even ROAS and MER, so you can judge any paid campaign with confidence.

GGrow.Media Editorial Team·Updated October 30, 2026·8 min read

Paid advertising dashboards throw a dozen acronyms at you, and most of them look important. In reality, a handful of metrics connect in a simple chain, and once you see that chain you can diagnose almost any campaign in a few minutes. This guide walks through each metric with a formula, a worked example, and the decision it should drive.

Key takeaways

  • Every core ad metric is a ratio of four raw numbers: spend, impressions, clicks, and conversions (plus revenue).
  • CPM and CTR tell you about your audience and creative; CVR tells you about your landing page and offer.
  • CPA is the metric to manage day to day, because it prices the outcome you care about.
  • ROAS only means something when you compare it to your break-even ROAS, which comes from your margin.
  • MER zooms out to total revenue over total marketing spend and keeps attribution arguments in check.

The four raw numbers behind every metric

Before memorizing formulas, notice that every ad metric is built from the same inputs. If you track these accurately, you can calculate everything else yourself, on any platform.

Raw number What it counts Where it comes from
Spend Money paid to the platform Ad platform billing
Impressions Times your ad was shown Ad platform
Clicks Times someone clicked through Ad platform
Conversions Purchases, leads, or signups Your analytics or checkout
Revenue Money from those conversions Your store or CRM

Throughout this guide, we will use one example campaign: you spend $1,000, earn 100,000 impressions, get 1,500 clicks, and record 45 purchases worth $3,150 in total revenue.

Reach and attention metrics: CPM and CTR

CPM (cost per mille)

CPM is the price of 1,000 impressions. It reflects how competitive and expensive your audience is to reach.

Formula: CPM = (Spend / Impressions) x 1,000

Example: ($1,000 / 100,000) x 1,000 = $10 CPM

CPM is mostly set by the auction: who else wants the same people, the time of year, and the platform. Ranges vary widely by platform, country, and season, so compare your CPM against your own history rather than against numbers you read online. A sudden jump during peak shopping seasons is normal.

CTR (click-through rate)

CTR is the share of impressions that turn into clicks. It is your best read on whether the creative and message stop the right people.

Formula: CTR = (Clicks / Impressions) x 100

Example: (1,500 / 100,000) x 100 = 1.5% CTR

A low CTR usually points to a creative or targeting problem. Before blaming the platform, test a new hook, a clearer headline, or a tighter audience. A high CTR on the wrong audience is not a win, so always read CTR alongside conversion rate.

Cost and conversion metrics: CPC, CVR, and CPA

CPC (cost per click)

Formula: CPC = Spend / Clicks

Example: $1,000 / 1,500 = $0.67 CPC

CPC is really a product of the two metrics above: CPC = CPM / (CTR x 10). That means you can lower CPC either by finding cheaper impressions or by improving CTR. Improving CTR is usually the lever you control.

CVR (conversion rate)

Formula: CVR = (Conversions / Clicks) x 100

Example: (45 / 1,500) x 100 = 3% CVR

CVR measures what happens after the click. If CTR is healthy but CVR is weak, the ad is doing its job and the landing page, offer, price, or checkout is not. Look for a mismatch between what the ad promised and what the page delivers.

CPA (cost per acquisition)

Formula: CPA = Spend / Conversions

Example: $1,000 / 45 = $22.22 CPA

CPA ties everything together: CPA = CPC / CVR. This is the number to watch daily. It tells you exactly what each customer or lead costs, and you can compare it directly against what that customer is worth.

Revenue metrics: ROAS and break-even ROAS

ROAS (return on ad spend)

Formula: ROAS = Revenue / Spend

Example: $3,150 / $1,000 = 3.15x ROAS (sometimes written as 315%)

ROAS looks like profit, but it is not. It measures revenue, and revenue still has to cover product costs, shipping, payment fees, and refunds before anything is left over.

Break-even ROAS

Break-even ROAS is the ROAS at which ad spend exactly equals the gross profit it generates. Below it, every sale loses money. Above it, you make money.

Formula: Break-even ROAS = 1 / Gross margin

Work through it in three steps:

  1. Calculate your gross margin per order: (Revenue - cost of goods - shipping - fees) / Revenue.
  2. Divide 1 by that margin, expressed as a decimal.
  3. Compare your actual ROAS against the result.

Example: If your gross margin is 40%, break-even ROAS = 1 / 0.40 = 2.5x. Your 3.15x campaign clears the bar. If your margin were 25%, break-even would be 4.0x and the same campaign would lose money.

Gross margin Break-even ROAS
20% 5.0x
30% 3.33x
40% 2.5x
50% 2.0x
70% 1.43x

This table explains why a digital product seller and a furniture store can run the same ROAS and get opposite results.

Target CPA from break-even

You can also flip the math. If your average order value is $70 and your margin is 40%, each order generates $28 in gross profit. That $28 is your break-even CPA. Any CPA below it is profitable on the first purchase. If customers buy repeatedly, you can justify a higher CPA, which is where lifetime value comes in. Zooming out: MER and blended performance

Platform-reported ROAS relies on attribution, and attribution is imperfect. Customers see an ad on one platform, search your brand on another, and buy after opening an email. Each platform may claim the same sale.

MER (marketing efficiency ratio) sidesteps that problem.

Formula: MER = Total revenue / Total marketing spend

If your business made $40,000 last month and spent $8,000 across all ads, tools, and sponsorships, MER is 5.0x. It does not tell you which channel worked, but it tells you whether marketing as a whole is pulling its weight.

Use the two together:

  1. Use platform ROAS and CPA to compare campaigns and creatives inside a platform.
  2. Use MER to decide whether to increase or cut total budget.
  3. If platform ROAS rises while MER falls, suspect over-attribution and look closer.

A five-minute diagnostic routine

When a campaign underperforms, walk the chain from top to bottom and stop at the first broken link.

  1. CPM rising? Your audience got more competitive or too narrow. Broaden targeting or wait out seasonal spikes.
  2. CTR falling? Creative fatigue or a weak hook. Refresh creative and test new angles.
  3. CVR falling? Check the landing page, page speed, price, stock, and message match.
  4. CPA above break-even? Fix the weakest link above before raising budget.
  5. ROAS fine but MER falling? Investigate attribution overlap and organic demand.
Symptom Likely cause First fix
High CPM, normal CTR Competitive auction Broaden audience, test placements
Normal CPM, low CTR Weak creative New hook, image, or headline
Good CTR, low CVR Page or offer mismatch Align page with ad promise
Good CVR, high CPA Expensive clicks Improve CTR to lower CPC

Your next step

Pull last month's spend, impressions, clicks, conversions, and revenue for one campaign, and calculate each metric in this guide by hand once. Then calculate your break-even ROAS from your real margin and write it at the top of your reporting sheet. From now on, judge every campaign against that line. Frequently asked questions

What is a good ROAS?

There is no universal good ROAS, because the number that matters is your break-even ROAS, which depends on your margins. A business with 80% gross margin can profit at a ROAS that would lose money for a business with 30% margin. Calculate your break-even first, then judge campaigns against it.

Should I optimize for CPC or CPA?

Optimize for CPA in almost every case, because it measures the cost of the outcome you actually want. A cheap click that never converts is worthless, while an expensive click that converts reliably can be your best traffic. Use CPC as a diagnostic, not a goal.

What is the difference between ROAS and MER?

ROAS measures revenue attributed to a specific campaign or platform divided by that campaign's spend. MER divides total revenue by total marketing spend across every channel, ignoring attribution entirely. ROAS helps you tune campaigns, while MER tells you whether your marketing as a whole is paying off.

Why do my platform numbers not match my analytics numbers?

Ad platforms and analytics tools use different attribution windows, count conversions differently, and lose some data to privacy settings and blocked tracking. Expect gaps between them. Pick one source of truth for decisions, document why, and watch the trend rather than chasing an exact match.
