How to Price Brand Deals and Negotiate Better Sponsorships

Learn how to price brand deals with rate formulas, CPM and flat-fee models, usage rights and exclusivity fees, negotiation scripts, and a contract checklist.

GGrow.Media Editorial Team·Updated October 30, 2026·9 min read
On this pageKey takeawaysKnow what you are actually sellingChoose a pricing modelCalculate your base ratePrice usage rights and exclusivity separatelyHow to negotiate with confidenceContract checklistYour next step

Pricing your first brand deal often feels like guessing, and many creators either undercharge out of fear or quote a number they cannot justify. The fix is a pricing system: a clear formula for your base rate, defined fees for extras like usage rights and exclusivity, and a negotiation approach that protects both your income and your relationship with the brand. This guide gives you all three, plus a contract checklist to review before you sign anything.

Key takeaways

  • Price based on what you deliver: audience reach, production work, usage rights, exclusivity, and your time.
  • A view-based CPM formula gives you a defensible base rate you can explain to any brand.
  • Usage rights and exclusivity are separate, billable extras, not free add-ons.
  • Negotiate scope, not just price; trade deliverables instead of discounting.
  • Never start work without a written agreement covering deliverables, payment, rights, and approvals.

Know what you are actually selling

A sponsorship is not just "a post." It bundles several kinds of value, and each one deserves a price.

  • Audience access. The number of relevant people who will see the content.
  • Content production. Scripting, filming, editing, and design work.
  • Creative trust. Your recommendation carries weight because your audience trusts you.
  • Usage rights. Permission for the brand to reuse your content elsewhere.
  • Exclusivity. Agreeing not to work with competitors for a period of time.
  • Time and revisions. Calls, drafts, approval rounds, and reporting.

When a brand asks for your rate, you are pricing this whole bundle. Breaking it apart makes your number easier to justify and easier to adjust.

Choose a pricing model

Different deals suit different models. Know them all so you can choose the right one for each brand.

Model How it works Best for Watch out for
Flat fee One price for a defined set of deliverables Most standard sponsorships Underpricing if scope grows
CPM (per 1,000 views) Price tied to expected or actual views Video integrations, newsletters Disputes over how views are counted
Performance-based Pay per sale, sign-up, or lead Brands with strong tracking You carry the risk if conversions are low
Hybrid Lower flat fee plus a performance bonus New brand relationships Complex tracking and reporting
Retainer or package Several pieces over months at a set rate Ongoing partnerships Committing to work you cannot deliver

For most creators, a flat fee calculated from a CPM formula is the clearest starting point, with performance bonuses as an optional extra. If terms like CPM and CPA are new to you, see our explainer on CPM, CPC, CPA, and ROAS.

Calculate your base rate

The CPM formula

Base rate = (average views per post ÷ 1,000) × your CPM

  1. Find your average views. Use the median views from your last 10 to 15 comparable posts, measured at a consistent point, such as 30 days after publishing. Using the median prevents one viral post from inflating your rate.
  2. Choose a CPM. Rates vary widely by platform, niche, and audience. Commonly cited ranges for dedicated video integrations often fall somewhere around $10 to $50 per 1,000 views, with high-value niches such as finance, business software, and technology toward the upper end. Treat this strictly as a starting point.
  3. Multiply. For example, a hypothetical creator averaging 20,000 views at a $25 CPM would have a base rate of $500 for one integration.

Adjust for the work involved

Then adjust the base rate for production and format:

  • Dedicated video or post (entirely about the brand): price higher than a short mention, as it takes more work and attention.
  • Short integration (a segment inside regular content): use the base rate.
  • Simple mention or story frame: price lower.
  • Complex production (travel, props, multiple locations): add your additional costs and time.

Price usage rights and exclusivity separately

These two extras are where many creators leave the most money on the table.

Usage rights

If a brand wants to run your content as a paid ad, post it on its own channels, or use it on its website, it is getting value beyond your audience. Charge for it, and always limit it by time and channel.

Exclusivity

Exclusivity prevents you from working with competitors, which can block other income. Price it based on how much potential work you are giving up, and define the category narrowly. "No other running shoe brands for 30 days" is reasonable; "no athletic brands for a year" is a very different ask.

Example add-on structure

There is no universal standard, but many creators price extras as a percentage of the base fee. Here is one illustrative structure to adapt:

Add-on Example pricing approach
Organic reposting on brand channels A modest flat fee or small percentage of base
Paid ad usage A percentage of base for each month of usage
Whitelisting (ads run from your account) A monthly fee, priced similarly to paid usage
Category exclusivity A percentage of base for each month, scaled by category breadth
Extra revision rounds A set fee per round beyond what is included
Rush delivery A surcharge for tight deadlines

Adjust the percentages to your niche and demand, and raise them as more brands approach you.

How to negotiate with confidence

A five-step negotiation process

  1. Ask questions first. Learn the brand's goals, timeline, deliverables, and budget before quoting. "What does success look like for this campaign?" tells you what to emphasize.
  2. Invite their budget. "Do you have a budget range in mind?" can save you from quoting below what they planned to spend.
  3. Quote packages, not single numbers. Offer two or three options at different levels so the conversation becomes which one, not whether.
  4. Trade, do not discount. If the budget is lower than your rate, remove deliverables, shorten usage rights, or drop exclusivity rather than doing the same work for less.
  5. Confirm everything in writing. Summarize the agreed terms in an email before any contract is drafted.

A counteroffer template

"Thanks for the offer. Based on my recent average of [X] views per video and the scope you described, my rate for one integration with 30 days of organic usage is [rate]. If your budget is fixed at [their number], I can do a shorter mention without usage rights. Happy to go with whichever option works best for your campaign."

This keeps the tone friendly, explains your reasoning, and gives the brand a path to yes.

Contract checklist

Review every agreement against this list before signing.

Item What to confirm
Deliverables Exact format, length, number of posts, and platforms
Timeline Draft deadline, posting date, and approval windows
Revisions Number of included revision rounds
Payment Amount, currency, due date, and any deposit
Usage rights Channels, duration, and whether paid ads are included
Exclusivity Category definition and duration
Disclosure How the sponsorship will be labeled, following the rules where you publish, such as FTC guidance in the US
Performance reporting Which metrics you will share and when
Cancellation Kill fee if the brand cancels after you start work
Content ownership Who owns the raw footage and final content

Red flags to watch for

  • Perpetual or unlimited usage rights for a one-time fee.
  • Payment terms that stretch far beyond the posting date with no deposit.
  • Vague deliverables such as "content as needed."
  • Requests to post without a clear sponsorship disclosure.

Your next step

Calculate your median views from your last 10 to 15 posts, choose a starting CPM for your niche, and build three package options with clear add-on fees for usage rights and exclusivity. Run the numbers through the sponsorship rate calculator, save the counteroffer template, and keep the contract checklist next to your inbox so you are ready the next time a brand reaches out.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge for a sponsored post?

A common starting point is to multiply your average views per post by a CPM rate that fits your niche, then adjust for production effort, usage rights, and exclusivity. Rates vary widely by platform, niche, and audience, so treat any formula as a starting point and refine it as you learn what brands accept.

Should I charge based on followers or views?

Views are usually the fairer measure because they reflect how many people actually see the content. Follower counts can overstate reach, especially on platforms where only a fraction of followers see each post. Many creators use recent average views and mention follower count only as context.

What are usage rights in a brand deal?

Usage rights give the brand permission to reuse your content beyond your own channel, such as in paid ads, on its website, or in email campaigns. Because that extends the value of your work, creators typically charge extra and limit the rights to a set time period.

Is it okay to negotiate a brand's first offer?

Yes. Brands generally expect negotiation, and a polite, well-reasoned counteroffer rarely costs you the deal. If the budget truly cannot move, negotiate the scope instead, such as fewer deliverables, shorter usage rights, or no exclusivity.
