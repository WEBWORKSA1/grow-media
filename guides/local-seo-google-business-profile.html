Local SEO: Optimize Your Google Business Profile Step by Step

A step-by-step local SEO guide to optimizing your Google Business Profile: categories, reviews, photos, NAP consistency, local pages and regular posts.

GGrow.Media Editorial Team·Updated October 30, 2026·8 min read

When someone searches for a service "near me," the map listings at the top of the results often get the call before any website does. Those listings come largely from Google Business Profile, and most local businesses leave much of theirs incomplete. This guide walks you through optimizing your profile and supporting it with the website and citation work that local search depends on.

Key takeaways

  • Local rankings broadly depend on relevance, distance, and prominence; you control relevance and prominence.
  • Your primary category is one of the most important choices on your profile, so pick it carefully.
  • A steady flow of genuine reviews, with thoughtful replies, builds both rankings and trust.
  • Your name, address, and phone number (NAP) must match exactly everywhere they appear online.
  • Local landing pages and regular profile posts reinforce what you offer and where.

How local rankings work

Google describes local ranking in terms of three broad factors. Understanding them tells you where to spend your time.

Factor What it means What you can do
Relevance How well your profile matches the search Accurate categories, services, description, website content
Distance How far you are from the searcher or the searched location Little, beyond setting a correct address or service area
Prominence How well known and trusted you are Reviews, citations, local links, mentions, active profile

Since distance is mostly fixed, every step in this guide aims at relevance or prominence.

Step 1: Claim, verify, and complete your profile

If you have not already, claim your profile and complete verification. Then fill in every field that applies:

  1. Business name: exactly as it appears on your signage and documents, with no added keywords.
  2. Address or service area: show your address if customers visit you; set a service area and hide the address if you travel to customers.
  3. Phone number: a local number you answer, matching your website.
  4. Website link: your homepage or the most relevant location page.
  5. Hours: regular hours plus special hours for holidays.
  6. Attributes: accessibility, payment options, amenities, and other details that apply.
  7. Description: up to 750 characters explaining what you do, who you serve, and what sets you apart, in plain language.

Tag your website link with UTM parameters so profile visits show up separately in your analytics. The UTM builder creates a clean link in seconds, for example with source set to "google" and medium set to "organic-local."

Step 2: Choose the right categories

Your primary category tells Google what kind of business you are, and it has an outsized effect on which searches you appear for.

  1. List the main services that drive your revenue.
  2. Search for your top service in your area and note the primary categories competitors in the map results use.
  3. Choose the most specific primary category that describes your core business, such as "Emergency veterinarian" rather than a broader animal-related category when that is truly what you are.
  4. Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely offer.
  5. Add individual services and products in their dedicated sections, with short descriptions.

Revisit categories once or twice a year, since available options change.

Step 3: Build a review engine

Reviews influence both rankings and buying decisions. The goal is a steady, ongoing flow of honest reviews rather than a one-time burst.

How to ask

  • Ask in person at the moment of satisfaction, such as when a job is finished or a customer thanks you.
  • Follow up with a short message containing your direct review link.
  • Add the link to receipts, invoices, and follow-up emails.
  • Make it easy: one tap to open the review form.

A simple request template:

Thanks again for choosing us today. If you have a minute, a short review helps other people in town find us: [review link]. We read every one.

Rules to follow

  • Never pay for reviews or offer discounts in exchange for them.
  • Do not ask only happy customers while filtering out others, a practice known as review gating.
  • Never write reviews for yourself or ask staff to.

How to reply

Reply to every review within a few days. Thank positive reviewers with a specific, personal note. For negative reviews, acknowledge the issue, apologize where appropriate, and invite the person to contact you directly to resolve it.

Step 4: Add photos and keep the profile active

Profiles with recent, real photos tend to look more trustworthy to customers. Upload a balanced set:

Photo type What to show
Logo and cover Clean, recognizable branding
Exterior Storefront from the street, so people can find you
Interior What customers see when they walk in
Team Real staff at work
Products or work Finished jobs, menu items, before-and-after shots

Use real photos, not stock images. Add new ones regularly, such as a few per month.

Profile posts

Posts let you share updates, offers, and events directly on your profile. They keep your listing fresh and give searchers a reason to choose you.

  1. Post at least a few times per month.
  2. Rotate between offers, seasonal tips, new work, and events.
  3. Include a clear call to action button, such as "Call" or "Book."
  4. Use the same photo and tone standards as the rest of your profile.

Step 5: Make NAP consistent everywhere

Your business name, address, and phone number appear across directories, social profiles, maps apps, and industry sites. These listings are called citations. Mismatches, such as an old phone number or "Street" versus "St." in one place and a former address in another, can confuse both customers and search engines.

  1. Decide on one exact format for your NAP and write it down.
  2. Update your website footer and contact page to match.
  3. Search for your business name and old phone numbers to find existing listings.
  4. Correct major directories, data aggregators relevant to your country, and industry-specific sites.
  5. Remove or merge duplicate listings.
  6. Recheck after any move or phone change.

Step 6: Build local landing pages

Your website supports your profile by confirming what you do and where. Create a dedicated page for each core service, and if you serve several towns, a page for each area with genuinely local content.

A strong local landing page includes:

  • A clear heading naming the service and location.
  • A description of how you deliver the service in that area.
  • Local proof: photos of real jobs, area-specific reviews, and neighborhoods served.
  • Your NAP, hours, and a map embed.
  • LocalBusiness structured data that matches your profile.
  • A prominent call or booking button.

Avoid swapping only the town name across dozens of near-identical pages. Thin duplicate pages rarely perform and can drag down the site. To find the phrases customers actually use, follow our keyword research step-by-step guide, and for the wider foundations, read SEO for beginners.

Your next step

Set aside one focused hour this week. Audit your profile against Step 1 and fill every empty field, confirm your primary category against local competitors, and upload ten real photos. Then create your review request message and send it to your five most recent satisfied customers. Put a recurring monthly reminder in your calendar to post updates, reply to reviews, and check citations.

Frequently asked questions

How long does local SEO take to show results?

Fixing profile basics such as categories, hours, and photos can affect visibility within weeks, while reviews and local authority build over months. Results depend heavily on how competitive your area and category are. Track progress monthly rather than daily.

Can I rank in a city where I do not have an address?

Your Google Business Profile is tied to your real location or service area, so the map results generally favor businesses near the searcher. You can still build helpful service-area pages that rank in regular search results for nearby towns. Never create fake addresses or virtual offices to appear local, which violates platform guidelines.

Should I respond to negative reviews?

Yes. A calm, specific, and professional reply shows future customers how you handle problems. Acknowledge the issue, avoid arguing or sharing private details, and offer a way to continue the conversation offline.

Do keywords in my business name help rankings?

Your profile name should match your real-world business name exactly. Adding extra keywords that are not part of your legal or signage name violates the guidelines and can lead to suspension. Put keywords in your description, services, posts, and website instead.
