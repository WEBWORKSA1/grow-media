Growth glossary

Plain-English definitions of the marketing, SEO, ads, email, analytics, AI and creator-economy terms you'll meet while growing.

ABCDEFHIKLMNOPRSTUW
A/B test
An experiment that shows two versions of something, such as a headline or email subject line, to similar groups at the same time to see which performs better. Changing one element at a time makes the result easier to interpret.
Activation rate
The share of new users, visitors, or subscribers who complete the first meaningful action that signals they got value, such as finishing setup or joining an email list. It shows whether first impressions turn into real engagement.
Affiliate marketing
Earning a commission by recommending another company's product through a tracked link or code. The creator or publisher is paid when a referred person buys or signs up.
Alt text
A short written description attached to an image in a web page's code. It helps people using screen readers understand the image and gives search engines context about what the image shows.
Answer engine
A search experience, often powered by a large language model, that responds to a question with a written answer rather than only a list of links. These answers may cite the sources they draw from.
Attribution model
The set of rules used to decide which marketing touchpoints get credit for a conversion. Common approaches include giving all credit to the last click, the first click, or spreading it across several interactions.
Average order value (AOV)
Total revenue divided by the number of orders over a period. Raising it, for example with bundles or add-ons, increases revenue without needing more customers.
Backlink
A link from another website pointing to yours. Search engines treat relevant links from trusted sites as a signal that your content is credible and worth ranking.
Bounce rate
The percentage of visits in which someone views a page and leaves without taking any further tracked action. Whether a high bounce rate is a problem depends on the page's purpose.
Brand deal
A paid agreement in which a business sponsors a creator to feature its product or message in their content. Terms typically cover deliverables, timing, disclosure, usage rights, and payment.
Break-even ROAS
The return on ad spend at which ad costs exactly equal the gross profit they produce, calculated as one divided by your gross margin. Campaigns below this level lose money on each sale.
Call to action (CTA)
The specific instruction that tells your audience what to do next, such as subscribe, book a call, or buy now. A clear, single call to action usually outperforms several competing ones.
Canonical tag
A line of code that tells search engines which version of a page is the main one when similar or duplicate versions exist. It helps consolidate ranking signals onto the preferred URL.
Churn rate
The percentage of customers or subscribers who cancel or stop engaging during a given period. Lowering churn is often one of the most efficient ways to grow recurring revenue.
Click-through rate (CTR)
The share of people who click a link, ad, or search result after seeing it, calculated as clicks divided by impressions. It reflects how compelling and relevant your message is to the people who see it.
Cohort analysis
A way of grouping customers or users by a shared starting point, such as the month they signed up, and tracking how each group behaves over time. It reveals whether retention and spending are improving for newer groups.
Context window
The maximum amount of text a large language model can consider at once, including your prompt, any pasted material, and its own response. Content beyond that limit is not taken into account.
Conversion rate (CVR)
The percentage of visitors or clicks that complete a desired action, such as a purchase or signup. It measures how well a page, offer, or funnel step persuades people to act.
Core Web Vitals
A set of measurements Google uses to assess real-world page experience, covering loading speed, responsiveness, and visual stability. Strong scores make pages feel faster and smoother for visitors.
Cost per acquisition (CPA)
The average amount spent on marketing to gain one customer, lead, or other conversion, calculated as spend divided by conversions. It is one of the most useful numbers for judging whether a campaign is profitable.
Cost per click (CPC)
The average price paid for each click on an ad, calculated as spend divided by clicks. A lower CPC is only valuable if those clicks still convert.
Cost per mille (CPM)
The cost of showing an ad 1,000 times, with mille meaning thousand in Latin. It mainly reflects how competitive and expensive it is to reach a particular audience.
Crawling
The process by which search engine bots discover pages by following links and reading sitemaps. A page that cannot be crawled is unlikely to be indexed or shown in results.
Creator economy
The ecosystem of independent creators who earn income from content and audiences, along with the platforms, brands, and tools that support them. Income sources include ads, sponsorships, products, and memberships.
Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
The total sales and marketing cost of winning one new customer over a period. Comparing it with customer lifetime value shows whether growth is sustainable.
Customer lifetime value (LTV)
An estimate of the total gross profit or revenue a typical customer generates over their entire relationship with your business. It sets an upper limit on how much you can afford to spend acquiring a customer.
DKIM
An email authentication method that adds a digital signature to outgoing messages so receiving servers can confirm the email really came from your domain and was not altered. It supports better deliverability.
DMARC
An email authentication policy that tells receiving servers what to do with messages that fail SPF or DKIM checks, such as deliver, quarantine, or reject them. It also lets domain owners receive reports about email sent in their name.
Double opt-in
A signup process in which a new subscriber must confirm their email address, usually by clicking a link, before being added to a list. It tends to produce cleaner lists and fewer spam complaints.
E-E-A-T
Short for experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness, a set of qualities described in Google's guidance for evaluating content quality. Content that demonstrates real first-hand experience and credible sourcing aligns with these qualities.
Engagement rate
The share of your audience that interacts with a piece of content through likes, comments, shares, saves, or clicks. Platforms and brands calculate it differently, so always state which formula you use.
Entity
A distinct, identifiable thing such as a person, brand, place, or product that search engines and AI systems can recognize and connect to facts. Describing your entity consistently across the web helps systems understand who you are.
Event
Any user action recorded by an analytics tool, such as a page view, button click, form submission, or purchase. Events are the raw building blocks of most modern analytics reports.
Evergreen content
Content that stays relevant and useful for a long time, such as tutorials and fundamentals, rather than news tied to a moment. It can attract steady traffic for months or years with periodic updates.
Featured snippet
A highlighted answer box shown near the top of some search results that quotes a short passage from a web page. Clear, direct answers under descriptive headings improve the chance of being chosen.
First-party data
Information you collect directly from your own audience and customers, such as email signups, purchase history, and survey responses. It is generally more reliable and privacy-friendly than data bought from third parties.
Funnel
A model of the stages people pass through on the way to becoming customers, from first discovering you to buying and returning. Measuring conversion between stages shows where people drop off.
Hallucination
When an AI model produces information that sounds confident and plausible but is false or made up, such as a non-existent statistic or source. It is the main reason AI-assisted content needs human fact-checking.
Hook
The opening line, image, or first few seconds of a piece of content designed to capture attention and make people keep reading or watching. Strong hooks state a clear payoff or create curiosity quickly.
ICE scoring
A simple prioritization method that rates each experiment idea on impact, confidence, and ease, then combines the scores to rank them. It helps small teams decide what to test first without lengthy debate.
Impression
A single instance of content or an ad being displayed on someone's screen. Impressions measure exposure, not attention or action.
Indexing
The process by which a search engine stores and organizes a crawled page so it can appear in search results. Pages can be crawled but still left out of the index if they are low quality or blocked.
Keyword difficulty
An estimated score from SEO tools indicating how hard it would be to rank on the first page for a search term. Each tool calculates it differently, so use it for relative comparison rather than as an exact measure.
Large language model (LLM)
A type of AI system trained on large amounts of text to predict and generate language. It powers AI assistants that can draft, summarize, translate, and answer questions.
Lead magnet
A free, useful resource such as a checklist, template, or mini course offered in exchange for someone's email address. The best lead magnets solve one specific problem quickly.
Leading indicator
A metric that changes before the result you care about and helps predict it, such as qualified leads predicting future sales. Leading indicators let you adjust course before final results arrive.
Long-tail keyword
A longer, more specific search phrase that typically has lower search volume but clearer intent and less competition. These phrases are often easier for newer sites to rank for.
Lookalike audience
An ad targeting option in which a platform finds new people who resemble an existing group, such as your customers or email subscribers. Its quality depends heavily on the quality of the source list.
Marketing efficiency ratio (MER)
Total revenue divided by total marketing spend across all channels for the same period. It gives a big-picture view of marketing performance without relying on attribution.
Media kit
A concise document that presents a creator or publisher's audience data, content formats, past results, and sponsorship packages to potential partners. It helps brands decide quickly whether a partnership fits.
Meta description
A short summary of a page written in its HTML that search engines may display under the title in results. It does not directly raise rankings but can influence whether people click.
North-star metric
The single metric that best captures the core value customers get from a business and that tends to predict long-term growth. It keeps a team focused on the outcome that matters most.
Open rate
The percentage of delivered emails that recipients open. Privacy features in some email apps can inflate or obscure opens, so it is best read alongside clicks and replies.
Organic reach
The number of people who see your content without paid promotion. It depends on platform algorithms, content quality, and how actively your audience engages.
Prompt
The instructions and information you give an AI assistant to produce a response. Specific prompts with context, constraints, and examples usually produce far better results than short, vague requests.
Retargeting
Showing ads to people who have already interacted with your website, app, or content but have not yet converted. It typically reaches warmer audiences than prospecting campaigns.
Retention rate
The percentage of customers, subscribers, or viewers who keep coming back over a set period. Strong retention means growth compounds instead of being replaced by constant new acquisition.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
A technique in which an AI system first retrieves relevant documents and then uses them to write its answer. It helps ground responses in specific sources and is common in AI search products.
Return on ad spend (ROAS)
Revenue generated by advertising divided by the amount spent on that advertising. It measures revenue efficiency, not profit, so compare it with your break-even ROAS.
Revenue per mille (RPM)
The amount a creator or publisher earns for every 1,000 views or page views, after any platform share is deducted. It is a practical way to compare earnings across content and channels.
Schema markup
Structured data code, often written in JSON-LD using the schema.org vocabulary, that labels page content such as articles, products, and businesses. It helps search engines and AI systems interpret your pages accurately.
Search intent
The underlying goal behind a search, such as learning something, comparing options, finding a specific site, or making a purchase. Matching content to intent is essential for ranking and conversions.
Segmentation
Dividing an audience or customer list into groups based on shared traits or behaviors, such as interests, purchase history, or engagement level. It allows more relevant messages for each group.
Sender reputation
The trust score that email providers assign to your sending domain and servers based on factors like complaint rates, bounces, and engagement. A strong reputation helps your emails reach the inbox.
SERP
Short for search engine results page, the page displayed after someone enters a search query. It can include standard links, ads, maps, videos, and AI-generated answers.
SPF
An email authentication record that lists which servers are allowed to send email on behalf of your domain. Receiving servers check it to help detect forged messages.
Title tag
The HTML element that sets a page's title, often shown as the clickable headline in search results and in browser tabs. A clear, specific title helps both rankings and click-through rate.
User-generated content (UGC)
Content such as reviews, photos, and videos created by customers or fans rather than the brand itself. The term also describes paid content made by creators in an authentic customer style for brands to use in ads.
UTM parameters
Tags added to the end of a URL, such as source, medium, and campaign, that tell analytics tools where a visit came from. Consistent naming is essential for reliable channel reporting.
Whitelisting
An arrangement in which a creator gives a brand permission to run paid ads using the creator's account name or content. Because it extends the content's reach and lifespan, it is usually priced separately from the original deal.
