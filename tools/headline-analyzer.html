Headline Analyzer

Headlines decide whether people click, read and share. This analyzer scores yours on patterns that consistently help: a sensible length, specific numbers, clear benefit words, speaking to the reader, and added context.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.

Results appear here as you type.

How the Headline Analyzer works

MetricFormula
Length6–12 words scores best
Specificitya number or timeframe adds points
Benefit and reader wordswords like guide, step-by-step, you, your
Penaltiesover 70 characters or ALL CAPS

Treat the score as a prompt to write better variations, not as the final word. Write ten versions, score them, then pick the two strongest and test them where you can: two thumbnail titles, two email subject lines, or two social posts. The best headline is specific, honest and easy to understand at a glance, and the content has to deliver on it.

Reference ranges

ScenarioValue
70–100Strong; test against one variation
50–69Decent; add a number or clearer benefit
Below 50Rewrite with a specific promise

Scoring is based on our own heuristic model.

FAQ

Does this work for YouTube titles and email subject lines?

Yes, the same principles apply. Keep subject lines shorter, since inboxes truncate earlier than search results.

Are clickbait headlines effective?

They may win a click but they lose trust, retention and repeat visits. Promise exactly what you deliver.

How many headline options should I write?

Write at least ten. The first few are usually the most obvious and least distinctive.
