LTV:CAC Calculator

The LTV:CAC ratio answers the most important growth question: is each new customer worth what it costs to acquire them? It applies to subscriptions, memberships, retainers and any business with repeat revenue.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.

Results appear here as you type.

How the LTV:CAC Calculator works

MetricFormula
Average lifetime (months)1 ÷ monthly churn
LTVARPU × gross margin × lifetime
LTV:CACLTV ÷ CAC
PaybackCAC ÷ (ARPU × gross margin)

A ratio around 3:1 is a widely used rule of thumb: high enough to cover overheads and still invest in growth. A very high ratio (above 5:1) can mean you're under-investing in acquisition. Payback period matters as much as the ratio for a small business, because cash spent on acquisition must come back before it runs out. Reducing churn is often the cheapest way to improve both numbers.

Reference ranges

ScenarioValue
Below 1:1Losing money per customer
1–3:1Fragile; fix retention or CAC
~3:1Healthy rule of thumb
5:1+Possibly under-investing in growth

Widely used rules of thumb; the right target depends on your cash position and growth stage.

FAQ

What counts as CAC?

All sales and marketing costs in a period divided by new customers acquired in that period, including ads, tools, content and sales salaries where relevant.

What if churn is near zero?

Cap lifetime at a realistic horizon (e.g. 36 months). Infinite lifetimes produce misleading LTV figures.

Does this work for one-time purchases?

Yes, if you replace monthly revenue and churn with average order value and expected number of repeat purchases.
