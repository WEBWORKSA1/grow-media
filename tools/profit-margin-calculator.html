Profit Margin & Markup Calculator

Margin and markup are the two numbers most small sellers confuse. Margin is profit as a share of the price; markup is profit as a share of the cost. This tool shows both, then subtracts shipping and acquisition costs so you see what you really keep from each sale.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.

Results appear here as you type.

How the Profit Margin & Markup Calculator works

MetricFormula
Gross margin(price − cost) ÷ price × 100
Markup(price − cost) ÷ cost × 100
Net profit per saleprice − cost − shipping − acquisition cost
Price for 50% net marginall costs ÷ 0.5

Pricing from markup alone is how sellers end up busy and unprofitable: a 100% markup sounds generous, but it's only a 50% gross margin, before shipping, payment fees, returns and ads. Run this for your top three products, then decide whether to raise prices, bundle items to lift order value, or cut fulfilment costs.

Exact conversions: margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup).

FAQ

What's the difference between margin and markup?

Markup is calculated on cost, margin on selling price. The same profit always gives a higher markup percentage than margin percentage.

What is a good profit margin?

It varies widely by industry. Many product businesses aim for gross margins of 50% or more so there is room for marketing and overheads.

Should I include payment processing fees?

Yes. Add them to fulfilment cost for an accurate net margin.
