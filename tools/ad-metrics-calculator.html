Ad Metrics Calculator (CPM, CPC, CPA, ROAS)

Ad dashboards report dozens of numbers. These six explain almost every campaign: what attention costs (CPM), what a visit costs (CPC), how compelling the ad is (CTR), how well the page converts (CVR), what a customer costs (CPA), and what you get back (ROAS).

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.

Results appear here as you type.

How the Ad Metrics Calculator works

MetricFormula
CPMspend ÷ impressions × 1,000
CPCspend ÷ clicks
CTRclicks ÷ impressions × 100
Conversion rateconversions ÷ clicks × 100
CPAspend ÷ conversions
ROASrevenue ÷ spend

Read the metrics as a chain. A high CPM with a good CTR can still be efficient; a cheap CPC with a poor conversion rate is not. When ROAS disappoints, find the weakest link: low CTR means the creative or offer isn't stopping the scroll, a low conversion rate points to the landing page or audience fit, and a high CPA with a healthy CTR and CVR usually means the audience is too expensive. Compare ROAS against your break-even ROAS (1 ÷ gross margin) rather than against 1.0.

Reference ranges

ScenarioValue
Break-even ROAS at 25% margin4.0x
Break-even ROAS at 50% margin2.0x
Break-even ROAS at 75% margin1.33x

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin. It is exact arithmetic, not a benchmark.

FAQ

What is a good ROAS?

Any ROAS above your break-even point is profitable before overheads. Many businesses with average margins aim for roughly 3x or more.

Is CTR or conversion rate more important?

Both matter, but they diagnose different problems: CTR reflects the ad, conversion rate reflects the landing page and offer.

Why does CPM vary so much?

CPM depends on competition for the audience, the time of year, placement and ad quality. Q4 and narrow, high-value audiences are usually more expensive.
